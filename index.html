History

The photograph above was taken by classmates I greatly admire during our visit to a museum in Dalian, China.

Between 1820 and 1945, the region now known as Dalian experienced Qing Empire, a brief British military invasion, Japanese massacre during the First Sino-Japanese War, Russian concession, and, after the Russo-Japanese War, four decades of Japanese colonization.

Even after 80 years under CCP governance, illicit advertisements in some of Dalian's less conspicuous corners have been observed soliciting prostitutes with the curious qualification: “Japanese speakers preferred.”

Jiadian LI (李伽典)

My research interests lie in applied microeconometrics and its interplay with political economy and economic history. I also explore how cultural factors, such as ideology and linguistics, interact with above contexts.

My recent work uses Japanese authorities' responses to infectious mortality in colonial Taiwan as a quasi-experiment to investigate relevant institutional consequences.

I am currently a joint Master's student at both Northwestern University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong. Prior to this, I completed my undergraduate studies in labor economics at Renmin University of China (with best thesis award).
